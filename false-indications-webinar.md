<!-- Thesis -->
<section class="section thesis-section" markdown="1">

A false indication is a diagnosis, not a verdict. Most handlers see the dog indicate where there's nothing, decide the dog is lying, and answer with a correction.

But "false indication" covers three different failures, and they don't share a fix. The dog can indicate where there is no target odor. The dog can indicate a real odor that isn't the target. Or the dog can give a true indication that you judge false — because the area was contaminated and the error is yours, not the dog's.

Name the type first. The response follows from the type.
{:.tagline}

This webinar gives you the framework to classify on your own dog.

</section>

<!-- The three types, visualised -->
<section class="section types-section" markdown="1">

## The three types
{:.section-title}

<ol class="types-chain">
  <li class="type-link"><span class="type-num">I</span><span class="type-name"><strong>No target odor.</strong> The dog indicates where the target odor isn't present. Drivers: overshadowing, handling, motivation.</span></li>
  <li class="type-link"><span class="type-num">II</span><span class="type-name"><strong>Wrong odor.</strong> The dog indicates a real odor that isn't the target — contact, similar, or animal odor; failure to localize the source.</span></li>
  <li class="type-link"><span class="type-num">III</span><span class="type-name"><strong>True, scored false.</strong> The dog is right and you mark it wrong — forgotten hides, contaminated objects, the human factor.</span></li>
</ol>

</section>

<!-- What you'll get -->
<section class="section" markdown="1">

## What you'll get
{:.section-title}

- **Type I — indication with no target odor.** The three drivers: overshadowing (the dog reads the object or the context, not the odor), handling (your control or pressure becomes the cue), and motivation (stress and a long blank search push the dog to offer something). How to tell them apart and what each one needs.
- **Type II — indication of the wrong odor.** Contact odor, similar odor, animal odor left by a previous dog; failure to localize the source; weak scent discrimination. Why rewarding an odor mixture when you're unsure builds this in — and how lineup maintenance and randomized reward take it back out.
- **Type III — a true indication you scored as false.** Forgotten hides, contaminated target objects, hands that placed without gloves. The human factor. Most "the dog is unreliable" cases live here.
- **The diagnosis, walked live on registrants' cases.** You bring the false indication; we classify it as a group and pick the response by type — wait it out, retrain discrimination, fix area management, or apply pressure.
{:.value-list}

</section>
